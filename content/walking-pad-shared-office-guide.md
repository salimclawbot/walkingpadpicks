## Comparison criteria

List every setting that changes between users and define who resets it. Each user must remain within the exact published capacity and other limits; never add a private safety buffer or average two users together. Accessibility and medical suitability require individual review.

## Turn a personal device into a governed shared asset

A machine that works for one owner can fail in a shared office because nobody controls setup, inspection, cleaning or access. Treat it like workplace equipment: establish an owner, approved location, eligible users, booking method, shutdown rule and incident path before purchase.

### Define the operating envelope

Capture exact model, region, manual revision, published capacity, permitted operating states, electrical requirements and clearances. The manufacturer's maximum capacity is a hard ceiling. Do not invent a percentage buffer or describe a higher number as safe. Users who do not fit every documented limit should not use that unit.

The [OSHA walking-working-surfaces overview](https://www.osha.gov/walking-working-surfaces) supports keeping walking areas orderly and addressing hazards, while the [computer workstation guidance](https://www.osha.gov/etools/computer-workstations/) helps with workstation geometry. Neither source approves desk treadmills or replaces an employer's risk process, disability accommodation or local regulation.

### Create a one-minute handoff

Post a short card: visually inspect; confirm the area is clear; retrieve the correct control; set the desk while stopped; follow startup instructions; stop for abnormal behavior; power down; clean only as directed; return the control; log any issue. Keep the full manual available through a stable link or printed copy.

### Separate access from pressure

Participation should be optional. Do not create step contests, weight-loss claims or productivity expectations that pressure staff to use powered equipment. Provide an ordinary seated or standing workstation path. Health and accessibility questions belong with the worker, employer and qualified professionals, not an affiliate guide.

### Preserve evidence after a problem

If a user reports a fall, abrupt speed change, smoke, overheating, electrical odor or damaged part, stop use and isolate the machine according to workplace procedure. Record the exact model, serial, time and observed facts without asking someone to recreate the event. Check the manufacturer and CPSC before any return to service.

A safety search is a dated check, not a lifetime guarantee. Search the exact brand and model in the [CPSC recall database](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls), review current [CPSC warnings](https://www.cpsc.gov/Warnings), and check [SaferProducts.gov](https://www.saferproducts.gov/) before purchase and periodically after it arrives. On April 16, 2026, CPSC warned consumers to stop using specified Sperax Pro, Q1, RM-01 and RM-02 walking pads and treadmills because of reported fall, burn and fire hazards. Those models are excluded from this guide. Follow any stop-use instruction immediately.

## Shared-office edge cases

Hot-desking can separate the machine from the desk preset, remote and cleaning record. Give each asset one visible identifier and keep its accessories together without blocking ventilation. A booking should reserve enough time for setup and shutdown; back-to-back use must not encourage hurried handoffs or skipped inspections.

Visitors and contractors should not infer permission from an unattended machine. Use the employer's access and induction process, and secure the device as the manual permits when unavailable. Child access is especially important in mixed-use spaces. Warning labels are not a substitute for controlled storage and supervision.

Procurement records should preserve the exact seller, serial, invoice, warranty contact and responsible manager. If the manufacturer publishes a firmware, manual or safety update, one owner must assess it and update the shared card. When the asset moves floors or buildings, rerun the electrical, clearance, noise and evacuation-route checks; approval in one location does not transfer automatically to another.

## Build an evidence workbook before shopping

Use one row per exact candidate, not per brand family. Capture the full model name, model or part number, regional voltage, selected colour or bundle when it changes components, manufacturer page, manual, current seller, return deadline and the date each source was checked. Screenshots should include the variant selector. A search-result snippet or review is a discovery aid, not sufficient evidence for a material limit.

Keep manufacturer facts, retailer facts and observations in separate columns. Manufacturer facts include documented dimensions, capacity, controls, warnings and permitted operating states. Retailer facts include the currently selected variant, seller, shipper, delivery estimate and return route. Observations include whether the machine fits the measured room and whether the planned handoff works. Never rewrite an observation as a universal product property.

### Resolve conflicts conservatively

When two official records disagree, do not select the value that makes a candidate look better. Save both records, contact the responsible manufacturer, and mark the field unknown until reconciled. The same rule applies when a marketplace title combines several generations or the manual lacks the retailer's claimed feature. Unknown is a useful decision result: it prevents a purchase from resting on an assumption.

### Evaluate the exact offer last

Only after a model passes identity, documentation, capacity, fit and current safety checks should the buyer inspect an offer. Confirm that the offer is for the same variant used in the worksheet, then note seller, shipper, availability, included parts and return terms. Price changes quickly, so this guide does not publish a static figure. An affiliate link is only a shopping path and never evidence that a model passed the checks.

### Keep the first trial reversible

Preserve packaging and follow the seller's trial conditions. Change one material variable at a time, use the machine only as the exact manual directs, and record pass, conditional, fail or unknown after ordinary use. Stop for unexpected acceleration or stopping, visible damage, unusual heat, smoke, burning odor, electrical problems, instability, a published warning, or any condition the manual identifies. Do not open the housing, bypass a safeguard or apply generic maintenance advice; contact the manufacturer or an appropriately qualified service provider.

### Update policy

Recheck links and safety status when the article is materially revised and immediately when a regulator or manufacturer publishes a relevant notice. Product names, manuals, apps, sellers and availability can change without warning. A dated audit makes that uncertainty visible and gives the next reviewer a reproducible starting point.
